Boat propulsion control and touchscreen dashboard

Propulsion and steering for a boat with two brushless motors. An MSP430 reads two control potentiometers and drives each motor's ESC with PWM. An ESP32 collects telemetry and feeds a 7-inch Nextion touchscreen that shows speed, battery voltage, temperature, and run time for each motor.

Role
Project lead
Dates
January 2026 to present
Status
Completed
Main parts
MSP430G2553, ESP32, Nextion NX8048P070, two 5–12S ESCs, BLDC motors
Power
36 V pack from two 18 V batteries

Design details

  • A buck converter steps the 36 V pack down to 5 V, and an LD1117 regulator supplies 3.3 V to the MSP430.
  • Separate main power and motor kill switches. The kill switch cuts supply to both ESCs as an emergency shutoff.
  • Two DRV5056 hall-effect sensors feed motor speed to the ESP32, and a DS18B20 reads temperature.
  • A 100 kΩ / 8.2 kΩ divider scales the 36 V pack to about 2.7 V for the ESP32's ADC.
  • A 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider on the display's serial line protects the ESP32's 3.3 V receive pin.
  • Bench-tested a 100 A AM32 ESC with a 1000–2000 µs PWM throttle signal.

Optical traffic counter for the City of St. George

A trailer-mounted counter that records turning-movement counts at intersections. One camera watches the intersection, and a YOLO model on a Jetson Orin Nano Super detects, tracks, and classifies road users by turning movement. Counts export to Excel, and data is delivered remotely over an LTE router.

Power and wiring design

  • Four 6 V golf-cart batteries wired 2S2P for 12 V and 372 Ah, charged by a 230 W solar panel through an MPPT controller and by the trailer's existing converter on shore power.
  • A timer-driven 40 A relay powers the system from 6:30 AM to 7:00 PM, so overnight draw stays at 4.5 W or less.
  • Boost converters supply 19 V to the Jetson and 24 V to a cabinet cooler, which a thermostat switches on above 85 °F.
  • Power budget of about 186 W with the cooler running and 55 W without. Every branch is fused at roughly 125–150% of its load.
  • Electronics sit on DIN rail in a 12 × 10 × 8 in IP66 enclosure, with LTE and GPS antennas on top.

Software

  • A Flask web dashboard shows live status, camera snapshots with counting zones, interval counts, and report downloads.

A 741 op-amp built from discrete transistors

I laid out the internal circuit of a 741 operational amplifier in KiCad and assembled it on its own PCB, one transistor at a time. The header follows the 741's 8-pin layout (offset null, inputs, supplies, output), so the board drops into any standard op-amp test circuit.

Course
ECE 2225, Lab 6
Layout
KiCad, hand-soldered through-hole
Date
October 2026
Status
Completed
Parts
20 BJTs, 11 resistors, 1 compensation capacitor
Bench result
Inverting amplifier at 1 kHz: 2.01 Vpp in, 3.74 Vpp out, a gain of about −1.86
  • Tested on the bench with a function generator, power supply, and oscilloscope, wired as an inverting amplifier on a breadboard.
  • An earlier practice board from the same course (Lab 5), also laid out in KiCad, to build up soldering and PCB workflow before the op-amp.
